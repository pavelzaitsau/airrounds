## Security assurance gap

AirRounds is receive-only in firmware. The radio starts in `WIFI_MODE_NULL`
with a promiscuous receive callback. The source has no frame-transmit or
deauthentication call. Manual source review confirms this, not radio
measurement. `make check` and the sanitizers pass but do not test for a transmit
call. The automated test that would fail the build on a transmit call is planned,
not built.

The independent RF measurement that would confirm radio silence on the
bench is deferred. Until it runs, the no-transmission property rests on manual
source review alone. On-air proof needs a separate monitor-capable receiver and
a repeat of the capture.

Corrected on 21 November 2026: the original report said a build gate confirmed
the receive-only property. No such gate exists, so this section now names
manual source review.

## Scan workflow hardware follow-up

The scan workflow was tested on November 19, 2026 with ESP32-S2 build
`f24e08d72046179b` and its matching Flipper package. Flipper scanned the
area and ran two selected points. Stop at **Captured 1/2** kept the first
result and left the interrupted point selected. The next round added
TELEPORT, saved its beacon, and showed **Not seen** for the disabled
hotspot. The original hotspot entry still missed after re-enabling it.
The hotspot had changed its access-point address. A fresh scan and an
explicit selection of the new row saved its beacon.

The Mac checked 13 PCAPs from seven rounds directly on the Devboard
microSD. Each file held one 802.11 beacon and matched its receipt and
session journal. TELEPORT's 284-byte PCAP from the continuation round
matched the filename, access-point address, channel, and byte count in
session `5fbb226d2286a6ad/00000005`, point 2. `tcpdump` independently
identified the TELEPORT beacon.

**Captured** in the tested build confirms a verified beacon PCAP on
microSD. The capture holds broadcast beacon frames for diagnostics and no
association or key-exchange traffic. The closure above applies to the
beacon acceptance criteria. The next UI wording is **Beacons** in the
list header and **Saved** on rows with verified beacon files.

## Beacons build microSD verification

On November 19, 2026, the Mac read five final journals and three PCAPs
from Devboard build `3eb6c250389cb5f6`, boot `0658fafa76960f5e`.
Every saved PCAP contained one raw 802.11 beacon. The file size, CRC-32
receipt, and journal agreed for all three. `tcpdump` independently
identified the TELEPORT and iPhone 12 Blue beacons.

| Check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Stop (`00000001`) | The journal recorded `stopped`. Point 1 saved a verified 281-byte PCAP. Stop interrupted point 2 after 292 ms. Its journal entry recorded `not_observed`, zero accepted beacons, and no PCAP or receipt. |
| TELEPORT (`00000002`, point 2) | 284-byte PCAP and receipt verified against the journal. The round was `complete`. |
| iPhone 12 Blue (`00000004` → `00000005`) | The original point completed its retry with `not_observed` after about 5 seconds. The hotspot returned with a different address on the same channel. Rescan and explicit selection produced a verified 385-byte PCAP and receipt. |

The stop and the completed miss have the same point outcome in the journal.
The round state and listening timestamps distinguish the two observations.
**Saved** in **Beacons** confirms a verified beacon PCAP on microSD.
